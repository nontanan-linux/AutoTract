Abstract—This study aims to construct a joint simulation
platform based on SUMO, CARLA, and Autoware, and apply the
platform to the investigation of multi-vehicle game scenarios at
unsignalized intersections. By leveraging the strengths of these
three open-source tools, we created an efficient environment for
modeling complex traffic scenarios and evaluating autonomous
driving algorithms with a low-code approach. The research
primarily focuses on reproducing deadlock situations
encountered by Robotaxis during public road tests and assessing
the impact of vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2X) cooperation on
traffic efficiency through comparative experiments. The results
demonstrate that the proposed platform effectively simulates
multi-vehicle interactions, serving as a valuable tool for
optimizing autonomous driving systems.

B. Architecture Design of the Joint Simulation Platform
The architecture design of the joint simulation platform is
crucial for conducting efficient multi-vehicle simulations, as
shown in Fig. 1. The platform integrates SUMO and CARLA
via the TraCI interface, then integrates CARLA and Autoware
through ROS (Robot Operating System), ensuring seamless
communication and data exchange. SUMO serves as the core
for traffic management, constructing the basic road network
and defining macroscopic traffic parameters. It generates
detailed road maps, including lane numbers, speed limits, and
turning rules, while allowing flexible settings for vehicle
departure times and route planning.

Data Interaction between CARLA and Autoware: Data
exchange between the two is achieved through ROS topics and
services. CARLA publishes sensor data, such as images
captured by cameras and point cloud data generated by radar,
to the corresponding topics according to ROS topic
specifications. Autoware subscribes to these topics, processes
the sensor data in its internal perception module. After
Autoware processes the sensor data and generates control
commands (such as acceleration, deceleration, and turning
commands), it sends these commands to CARLA via ROS
services. Upon receiving the control commands, CARLA
converts them into actual control signals for the vehicles,
enabling them to drive in the simulation environment.

D. Benefits of the Joint Simulation Platform
Scholars have tried to build joint simulation platforms such
as SUMO-CARLA or CARLA-Autoware [9]. However, these
platforms are typically constructed in a Linux environment,
which can be cumbersome for system setup and maintenance.
We have successfully established a tripartite joint simulation
platform integrating SUMO, CARLA, and Autoware under the
Windows 11 environment, specifically utilizing the Windows
Subsystem for Linux (WSL) within Windows 11 for Autoware
installation. This setup provides researchers with a highly userfriendly simulation environment without the need for extensive
code development. Additionally, since all three simulation
tools are open-source, researchers can customize and develop
their own code for more complex simulations.

Data exchange between SUMO and CARLA occurs via the
TraCI interface, ensuring real-time synchronization. SUMO
sends traffic flow information to CARLA, which adjusts its
simulation accordingly. Feedback regarding vehicle collisions
or trajectory deviations is relayed back to SUMO to update
traffic statistics. Communication between CARLA and
Autoware employs ROS topics and services, allowing for
efficient data processing and control signal transmission.

Building a simulation platform in our approach offers
numerous advantages. The key benefits are as follows:
1) User-Friendly Interface. This provides an intuitive user
interface that simplifies the setup and maintenance of the
simulation platform. Users can easily set up the simulation
environment without delving into the complex configurations
of the Linux system. This ease of use reduces the learning

various simulation attempts. In the environment of the

2) Efficient Resource Management and Performance
Optimization. The platform supports efficient resource
management, enabling flexible allocation of CPU, memory,
and storage resources to ensure the smooth operation of

3) Powerful Graphics Processing Capabilities. It supports

A. Experimental Scenario
The experimental scenario was designed to simulate a Tjunction located within the CARLA Town01 map, intentionally
devoid of any traffic signals to replicate real-world
unsignalized intersections. This choice of setting is crucial as
unsignalized intersections present unique challenges for
autonomous vehicles, primarily due to the lack of explicit
traffic control and the need for vehicles to negotiate right-ofway among themselves. In this scenario, vehicles approach the
intersection from multiple directions, creating complex
interactions influenced by factors such as vehicle speed, arrival
times, and driver behavior.

4) Flexibility of Open-Source Software. Simulation tools
such as SUMO, CARLA, and Autoware are all open-source,
allowing users to customize and develop them according to
their needs. In the Windows environment, the combination of
this flexibility and ease of use provides researchers with
powerful tool support.
In summary, the proposed approach not only provides
efficient resource management, powerful graphics processing
capabilities, and a user-friendly interface but also enhances
system flexibility and compatibility through the WSL feature.
And the whole platform can be easily migrated from one
machine to another using the import and export features of
WSL. Fig. 2 illustrates the visual effects of the co-simulation
platform during operation. The three open-source software
tools have achieved synchronization of the simulation interface.

To enhance the realism of the simulation, various traffic
conditions were introduced, such as varying vehicle types,
including passenger cars, buses, and trucks. Each vehicle type
has distinct characteristics which are configured via SUMO.
Additionally, the simulation included rainy environmental
conditions which were set up by CARLA.
B. Experimental Groups
Two distinct experimental groups were established to
evaluate the impact of V2X cooperation on traffic efficiency at
the unsignalized intersection. The first group, designated as the
non-cooperation group, consisted of vehicles that relied solely
on their own onboard sensors and algorithms for decisionmaking. These vehicles were unable to communicate with each
other or gain insights into the overall traffic state, which often
led to inefficient responses during critical moments at the
intersection. For example, when multiple vehicles approached
the intersection simultaneously, they frequently encountered
deadlock situations, severely hampering traffic flow.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This study successfully established a joint simulation
platform utilizing SUMO, CARLA, and Autoware, enabling
comprehensive analyses of multi-vehicle interactions at
unsignalized intersections. The results confirm that V2X
cooperation technology can effectively mitigate deadlock
issues and enhance overall traffic efficiency. The joint
simulation platform represents a robust tool for researching and
optimizing autonomous driving systems, with potential
applications in more complex traffic scenarios and algorithm
validation. Future work may extend this platform to explore the
performance of various autonomous driving algorithms in
increasingly complex environments and investigate additional
advanced technologies to further improve traffic efficiency and
safety. In the future, by creating a comprehensive array of
scenarios using the presented joint simulation platform, we can
thoroughly evaluate the performance of autonomous driving
algorithms in managing traffic conflicts, ultimately providing
insights into their effectiveness in real-world applications.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL ANALYSIS
What is important here is the fact that by using the
proposed co-simulation platform, it is possible to conduct
relatively detailed experimental analysis and quickly validate
ideas without the need for extensive code development.
A. Comparison of Traffic Efficiency
The experimental results revealed notable differences in
traffic efficiency between the two groups. In the absence of
V2X cooperation, the frequency of deadlocks increased
significantly as vehicle numbers rose.
In contrast, with V2X cooperation, traffic remained
relatively smooth even with the increased vehicles, highlighting
V2X technology's potential to enhance traffic efficiency
through improved vehicle coordination.
B. Typical Scenario Analysis
To further illustrate the benefits of V2X cooperation, a
series of typical scenarios were constructed using our joint
simulation platform, each reflecting common situations
encountered at unsignalized intersections. One particularly
telling scenario involved four autonomous vehicles attempting
to enter the intersection simultaneously, which resulted in a
deadlock in the non-cooperation group. The vehicles, lacking
communication, were unable to negotiate right-of-way
effectively, leading to an longer average waiting time for each
