Synergistic Control Strategy for Enhanced
Anti-Jackknifing Stability in Distributed-Drive
Articulated Trucks
Yang Tian , He Ma , Yahui Zhang , Member, IEEE, Guilin Wen ,
Xiangyu Wang , and Liang Li , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract— Electro-hydraulic compound steering systems
(EHCSSs) adopted by distributed-drive articulated trucks
(DATs) provide the probability of further realizing active
steering safety (ASS). However, due to the DATs’ articulated
mechanical structure characteristics, a singular control strategy
frequently cannot effectively solve lateral stability problems
such as jackknifing. Hence, this article proposes a novel
control strategy integrating active steering, torque vectoring,
and differential braking to suppress the jackknife with
improved lateral stability and less longitudinal speed loss.
First, a nonhierarchical dynamic model fused with EHCSS is
established, and the Artemisinin optimization algorithm (AOA)
is then applied to model parameter majorization. Afterward,
an advanced strategy is developed to suppress the jackknife of
DATs. Specifically, the proposed anti-jackknifing control strategy
is realized through the active steering of the front tractor wheel
to track the ideal trajectory, the torque vectoring control of the
rear tractor wheel, and the differential braking of the trailer.
Finally, hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) experiments are performed
to prove the validity and feasibility. The experiment results
illustrate that the proposed strategy is markedly effective in
anti-jackknifing. Compared to the control groups, the maximum
slip angles of the tractor and trailer are improved by 47.86%
and 84.26%, respectively, and the maximum yaw rates are
improved by 70.20% and 51.71%.
Index Terms— Active steering, anti-jackknifing control strategy, Artemisinin optimization algorithm (AOA), differential
braking, distributed-drive articulated trucks (DATs), torque vectoring.

stability, improved energy efficiency, and superior performance in challenging conditions [3]. However, due to the

DATs compared to passenger cars, there are growing safety
concerns associated with the operation of DATs [4], [5]. The
articulated mechanical structure of DATs makes them particularly susceptible to jackknifing, which can greatly affect their
lateral stability while driving [6]. Jackknifing is a hazardous
event in which the trailer of an articulated vehicle swings
sharply toward the tractor, resembling the motion of a folding
knife [7]. Path tracking control provides effective solutions to
mitigate these issues and ensure the safe operation of DATs,
a critical topic in both industry and academia [8], [9], [10].
Automotive original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) have
developed more efficient control subsystems for path-tracking,
including active front steering systems [11], active rear
steering systems [12], and torque vectoring control systems
[13]. Several scholars have implemented the electro-hydraulic
compound steering system (EHCSS) for active steering control [14], [15]. The EHCSS combines the advantages of
electric and hydraulic steering systems, offering active steering
capabilities for DATs [16]. Deploying the EHCSS is essential
for ensuring effective lateral stability control of DATs in
challenging operational environments and precise trajectory
tracking. However, integrating multiple control strategies with
the EHCSS to address jackknifing and stability challenges in
DATs under varying operational conditions remains largely
unexplored.
The combined control strategy of differential braking and
active steering has been demonstrated to enhance the lateral
stability of DATs [17]. The controller in [18] employs model
predictive control (MPC), using differential braking to conservatively generate additional yaw moments while employing
active steering for precise trajectory tracking. By integrating
the active steering and differential braking systems, [19]
enhances the directional stability of vehicles that turn at high
speeds on various road conditions. Additionally, [20] establishes a reconfigurable controller, which integrates multiple
strategies of active steering, torque vector, and differential
braking. Choi et al. [21] develop a coordinated control of
motor-driven power steering torque overlay and differential
braking under extreme conditions. In [22], an improved torque
vector control and differential braking strategy are proposed.

Although the aforementioned control strategies address vehicle control in lateral instability, little attention was paid to
analyzing the structural characteristics of DATs and proposing
a targeted solution to effectively and efficiently suppress the
jackknifing issues.
Designing an effective control strategy for the lateral stability of DATs requires coordinating the interactions between
steering, torque vector control, and differential braking while
quickly and accurately tracking the ideal trajectory [20], [24].
The structural complexity of the EHCSS presents challenges
for coordinated control [14], [25]. A layered time-delay robust
control strategy is proposed in [26] to ensure the safety of
SbW vehicles. However, hierarchical control design may cause
information delays and inaccurate coordination between multiple control strategies, leading to inefficiency [27]. Tian et al.
[28] establish a nonhierarchical model, designing a preset
performance controller that considers the sideslip angle and
yaw rate. Based on a non-hierarchical sensing control structure, a decentralized control scheme is designed in [29]
for motion and formation control tasks. Wang et al. [30]
employ an adaptive genetic algorithm to optimize the model,
enhancing its responsiveness. Therefore, to achieve precise
trajectory tracking and minimize system response time while
integrating multiple strategies, it is essential to establish a
non-hierarchical model that incorporates both the vehicle and
steering systems [31].
The key contributions of this research are as follows.

1) A novel anti-jackknifing control strategy is proposed
through the active steering of the tractor front wheel,
the direct yaw moment control of the tractor rear wheel,
and the differential braking of the trailer. Compared
to single control strategies, the proposed control strategy achieves more accurate front wheel angle tracking,
smaller sideslip angle, and yaw rate with less longitudinal speed loss.
2) A novel model fusing a 3 DoF model and an EHCSS
model is developed, integrating the Artemisinin optimization algorithm (AOA) for optimizing tire cornering
stiffness and an improved fitness function for accurate
parameter identification, effectively addressing the limitations of rigid hierarchical structures, allowing for faster
system response and improved stability control.

Before designing the control strategy, it is essential to
ensure that the dynamic model accurately reflects the operating
characteristics of the DATs. Among the parameters affecting
this model, the lateral stiffness of the tires plays a critical role
in determining side-slip and yaw responses. To solve this issue,
an optimization process will be conducted before developing
the control strategy.
In the optimization section, the selection of tire cornering
stiffness is based on the AOA to ensure that the model parameters accurately match the Trucksim simulation data. The
chosen range considers model fitting accuracy, global search
and local optimization strategies, and engineering feasibility,
ensuring that the optimized parameters enhance system stability while complying with actual vehicle design standards [33].

The insufficient lateral force from the tractor’s rear axle
causes mutual interaction between the torques generated by the
tractor and the trailer. The tractor generates a torque around
the hinge point, rotating clockwise, while the trailer generates
an opposing torque, rotating counterclockwise. This interaction leads to the “jackknifing” phenomenon of the vehicle,
as illustrated in Fig. 5(b). The resulting torque imbalance at
the connection point (typically the hinge) misaligns the driving
trajectories of the tractor and trailer, potentially leading to
severe vehicle instability or loss of control.
A. Controller Design

IV. C ONTROL S TRATEGY
In this section, a novel anti-jackknifing control strategy is
proposed. This strategy utilizes active steering of the front
tractor wheels with the EHCSS, torque vectoring of the rear
tractor wheels, and differential braking of the trailer.
As shown in Fig. 5(a), when the lateral force exerted on
the rear axle of the tractor reaches saturation, the rear axle
will slip in the direction of the lateral force. Assuming the
lateral force is directed to the left under these circumstances,
and the front axle of the tractor remains unaffected by slip, the
instantaneous rotation center of the tractor O1 is determined
using the vertical line method. Subsequently, the tractor rotates
clockwise around this rotation center, causing the trailer saddle
to rotate around the same instantaneous rotation center. The
instantaneous steering center of the trailer is O2 .

MPC is fundamentally based on the current state variables
and the system model to predict the evolution of state variables
over a specified future time horizon. The control strategy
involves formulating an objective function, which is typically
a cost function, while incorporating system constraints. The
optimal control sequence is then determined by solving this
optimization problem at each time step. In this section, the
MPC controller is designed according to the non-hierarchical
model established in Chapter II.
To link the modeling framework with controller design, the
MPC state vector and control inputs are directly derived from
the nonhierarchical dynamic model. The selected states reflect
the yaw and sideslip dynamics and are highly observable and
controllable within the EHCSS-integrated DATs system. The
control inputs correspond to front-wheel steering, rear electric
yaw moment, and trailer differential braking. This direct mapping reduces delay and improves response accuracy. Unlike
hierarchical models, the non-hierarchical approach enables
unified dynamic prediction, enhancing real-time performance
and system stability. Algorithm 2 provides the pseudocode for
the MPC-based anti-jackknifing control.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this study, an integrated control strategy is developed
and implemented in the DAT’s stability control to suppress
jackknifing under extreme conditions. Specifically, the proposed strategy is realized through the active steering of the
tractor’s front wheel, the torque vectoring of the tractor’s rear
wheel, and the differential braking of the trailer. By integrating
the EHCSS model into the DATs 3 DoF model, a new nonhierarchical model is established. The AOA is used to identify
the parameters of this hierarchical model. The optimization
results demonstrate that the new model is effective for developing active steering functions based on EHCSS. Additionally,
