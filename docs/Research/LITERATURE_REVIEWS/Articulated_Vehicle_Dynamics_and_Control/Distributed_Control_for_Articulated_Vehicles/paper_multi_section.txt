Abstract—This paper proposes a distributed cooperative
control strategy to address the accuracy of trajectory following
control of six-axle articulated vehicles with three-section
formation. Leveraging a kinematic-derived dynamic model, the
strategy employs combined Extended Ackermann feedforward
and Fuzzy PID control for the 4th and 6th axles, mitigating large
lateral deviations via dynamic PID parameter optimization.
Standard Fuzzy PID controls the 2nd, 3rd, and 5th axles. Cosimulation results using TruckSim and Simulink demonstrate
significant reductions in critical axle lateral deviations compared
to conventional PID, alongside effective management of other
axles under typical road conditions. The proposed approach
exhibits high precision and robustness, offering an effective
solution.
Keywords—Articulated vehicle, Fuzzy PID control, Trajectory
tracking, Multi-axle cooperative control

challenges to the development of control systems. Especially
in realizing high-precision trajectory following control,
traditional control methods are often difficult to meet its high
requirements for stability and accuracy. Therefore, the study of
multi-axis cooperative control strategies for six-axis articulated
vehicles to realize accurate trajectory following control not

Trajectory following control is one of the core technologies
in automatic driving and intelligent transportation systems, and
its goal is to enable the vehicle to follow a predetermined path
while maintaining a stable attitude and speed. For a six-axis
articulated bus vehicle, due to its multi-axis articulated
structure, the coordinated motion between the axes is
especially critical in the driving process. Traditional trajectory
following control methods are mostly designed for single-axis
vehicles or simplified models, which are difficult to be directly
applied to the control requirements of such complex systems.
In recent years, with the rapid development of control theory
and computational technology, advanced control strategies
based on model predictive control, sliding mode control, fuzzy
control and other advanced control strategies are increasingly
widely used in vehicle trajectory following control, which
provide new theoretical support and technical paths for the
control system design of six-axis articulated bus vehicles.Dasol
Jeong [3] et al. designed a pipelined robust model predictive
control, which is effectively applied to the control system of
six-axis articulated bus vehicles through pipeline analysis and
constraint tightening techniques to effectively deal with
modeling errors and uncertainties, and to achieve highprecision trajectory following control of a 3-axis articulated
vehicle.Mateusz Szumilas [4] et al. focused on the joint control
of articulated buses, and adjusted the damping of the joints
through the use of MEMS sensors and electro-hydraulic servo
systems to ensure the stability and maneuverability of the
vehicle at different speeds.Rui Li [5] et al. proposed a trajectory
following algorithm based on adaptive sliding mode control for
articulated vehicle control. By verifying in a hardware-in-theloop simulation environment, the ASMC method is able to
effectively control the lateral position deviation, heading angle
deviation and curvature deviation, and it reduces the errors and
oscillations and improves the following performance compared
with the traditional method. Peng Jing [6] et al. proposed an
autonomous guidance and trajectory following control system,
which realizes the path following of the Smart Tram by using
the method of sectional control. Li Si Xu [7] et al. proposed a
multi-point pre-scanning path following method for articulated
vehicles based on predictive control of dynamics model, which

is proposed for a three-section six-axis vehicle. This

Design of control strategy based on fuzzy PID
Traditional regulators—whether PID, LQR, or slidingmode schemes—tend to falter when confronted with the
pronounced nonlinearities inherent to multi-unit vehicles; their
fidelity hinges on precise plant models or they become fragile
in the face of sensor noise. Fuzzy inference, by comparison,
translates linguistic rules into control actions, thereby
accommodating uncertain parameters and strong nonlinear
couplings without recourse to an exact mathematical
description. A hybrid fuzzy-PID architecture is consequently
adopted to exploit this resilience, allowing gains to be reshaped
on-line as road friction, load distribution, or articulation angles

high gain increases in the fuzzy PID controller.

Control Policy Assignment
Vehicles are susceptible to a variety of disturbing factors
during turn-by-turn driving, which may exacerbate the
trajectory deviation of vehicles. In order to enhance the
robustness of three-knuckle six-axle intelligent rail train in
trajectory following deviation control, this paper proposes an
optimized control strategy allocation scheme (distributed
cooperative control). In this study, a distributed cooperative
control strategy is proposed to enhance the trajectory tracking
accuracy of six-axle articulated vehicles. Unlike centralized
steering control, where a central controller governs all axle
angles, the distributed control framework adopts a modular
approach. Each axle or axle group is equipped with an
independent local controller that receives coordination signals
from a supervisory controller. These local controllers execute
control tasks (e.g., fuzzy PID adjustment or feedforward
compensation) based on their respective lateral deviation
inputs.The key advantage of this framework lies in its fault

faced by three-unit six-axis articulated vehicles in complex
trajectory following. The core innovation of the strategy is that
the extended Ackermann feed-forward control is integrated to
predict the ideal corner for the 4th and 6th axes, which have
significant lateral deviations, and combined with a fuzzy PID
controller for dynamic error compensation; at the same time,
the standard fuzzy PID control is applied to the 2nd, 3rd, and
5th axes. The key research results are confirmed by the joint
simulation of TruckSim and Simulink: compared with the
traditional PID control, the proposed synergistic strategy
significantly improves the trajectory following accuracy of the
key articulated axes, effectively suppresses the lateral deviation,
and takes into account the stability of each part of the vehicle.
This study not only verifies the feasibility and superiority of
the distributed hybrid control architecture, but also provides a
validated control scheme with good robustness and application
prospects for solving the high-precision path navigation
problem of similar long, multi-unit articulated vehicles.
